# DriftSense Post-Phase Likert Questionnaire

Use the Phase 1 questionnaire immediately after Phase 1 and the Phase 2
questionnaire immediately after Phase 2. Responses concern the participant's
own experience of task alignment; they are not measurements of objective
attention or productivity.

## Response scale

Use the same five-point scale for every statement:

1. Strongly disagree
2. Disagree
3. Neither agree nor disagree
4. Agree
5. Strongly agree

Record `not applicable` separately. Do not replace it with the midpoint.

## Phase 1: extension without model-assisted check-ins

1. Starting a task in DriftSense made my intended task clear to me.
2. Starting a task in DriftSense helped me approach the task intentionally.
3. The end-of-session reflection helped me notice whether I had stayed aligned
   with my intended task.
4. Using DriftSense helped me stay aligned with my intended browser tasks.
5. DriftSense added effort to my normal work.
6. DriftSense was easy to use.
7. I was comfortable with the privacy of the information DriftSense recorded.
8. I would continue using this version of DriftSense.

## Phase 2: extension with model-assisted check-ins

Before the Likert items, ask:

**Do you remember seeing at least one mid-session check-in during Phase 2?**

Record `yes`, `no`, or `not sure`. If the answer is `no` or `not sure`, record
items 2--9 as `not applicable`.

1. DriftSense was easy to use during Phase 2.
2. The check-ins appeared at appropriate times.
3. The check-ins helped me reconsider whether I was still following my intended
   task.
4. The check-ins helped me stay aligned with my intended task.
5. The wording of the check-ins felt neutral.
6. The number of check-ins was acceptable.
7. The check-ins interrupted useful work.
8. The check-ins were annoying.
9. I preferred the Phase 2 version to the Phase 1 version.
10. I would continue using the Phase 2 version of DriftSense.

## Simple analysis

- Report the response count and percentage for each option, plus the median and
  interquartile range for each statement.
- Keep `not applicable` responses separate and report how many participants did
  not remember receiving a Phase 2 check-in.
- Report negatively worded burden items separately; do not combine these items
  into an unvalidated overall impact score.
- Treat Phase 1--Phase 2 ratings as descriptive perceived-impact evidence. The
  randomized comparison of eligible Phase 2 sessions remains the main causal
  test of the reflective check-in.
